## Autosomal Recessive Polycystic Kidney Disease

**Lead / Summary**

Autosomal recessive polycystic kidney disease (ARPKD) is a rare, inherited disorder characterized by the development of cysts in the kidneys and liver [1]. Unlike other forms of polycystic kidney disease, ARPKD typically manifests in infancy or childhood and can lead to serious complications [1]. The condition stems from genetic mutations affecting a crucial protein involved in the normal development and function of these organs [1].

ARPKD requires lifelong management and can significantly impact quality of life. Early diagnosis and treatment are critical to managing symptoms and slowing the progression of the disease [1]. While there is no cure, advancements in medical care have improved the outlook for individuals with ARPKD [1].

**Overview / Definition**

Autosomal recessive polycystic kidney disease (ARPKD) is a rare genetic disorder that primarily affects the kidneys and liver [1]. It is defined by the formation of fluid-filled cysts in the kidneys [1]. The presence of these cysts disrupts the normal structure and function of these organs, leading to various complications [1].

**Signs & Symptoms**

The signs and symptoms of ARPKD can vary in severity and may appear at different stages of life, sometimes even before birth [1]. Early signs can be detected during pregnancy or in the first few months after birth [1]. Common symptoms include enlarged kidneys, high blood pressure, urinary tract infections, abdominal pain, and liver problems [1]. In severe cases, a fetus may exhibit oligohydramnios (low amniotic fluid) and Potter sequence, which can lead to pulmonary hypoplasia (underdeveloped lungs) [1].

**Genetics & Pathophysiology**

ARPKD is caused by mutations in the *PKHD1* gene [1]. This gene provides instructions for making a protein called fibrocystin, which is found in the epithelial cells of both the renal tubules and the bile ducts [1]. Fibrocystin plays a role in the normal development and function of these structures [1]. When the *PKHD1* gene is mutated, it leads to a deficiency in functional fibrocystin [1]. This deficiency results in the characteristic polycystic dilation of the renal tubules and bile ducts, leading to cyst formation [1]. ARPKD is inherited in an autosomal recessive manner [1]. This means that both parents must carry a mutated copy of the *PKHD1* gene for their child to develop the condition [1]. If both parents are carriers, there is a 25% chance with each pregnancy that their child will have ARPKD [1].

**Management / Treatment**

Currently, there is no cure for ARPKD, and treatment focuses on managing symptoms and slowing the progression of the disease [1]. Management strategies include blood pressure control through medication, diet, and exercise [1]. Infants with underdeveloped lungs may require artificial ventilation [1]. Kidney failure may necessitate dialysis, and underweight infants may benefit from nutritional therapy [1]. In cases where the disease progresses to kidney failure, a kidney transplant may be necessary [1].

**Prognosis / Natural History**

ARPKD can be a serious condition, and about half of ARPKD patients develop end-stage renal failure within the first two decades of life, requiring renal replacement therapy such as dialysis or kidney transplantation [1]. Hepatic function must be closely monitored, as portal hypertension, splenomegaly, and cholangitis are common, especially in older patients [1]. The prognosis varies depending on the severity of the disease and the presence of complications. Ongoing medical care and management are essential to improve the quality of life and overall survival for individuals with ARPKD [1].

**Epidemiology**

ARPKD is considered a rare genetic disorder [1].
