# Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy: Research Report for Wikipedia Article

## Executive Summary

*   **Genetic Basis:** Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD) is caused by mutations in the *DMD* gene, which encodes for dystrophin, a protein crucial for muscle fiber integrity. These mutations are often deletions or duplications and follow an X-linked recessive inheritance pattern, primarily affecting males. (*Source: GeneReviews: Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy*)
*   **Diagnosis:** Diagnosis involves a combination of clinical evaluation, blood tests (elevated creatine kinase), and genetic testing to identify mutations in the *DMD* gene. Muscle biopsies may also be performed to assess dystrophin levels. (*Source: GARD: Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy*)
*   **Treatment:** Current treatment strategies focus on managing symptoms and slowing disease progression. Corticosteroids are a mainstay of treatment, while newer therapies like exon-skipping drugs target specific mutations. Physical therapy, occupational therapy, and respiratory support are also essential. (*Source: FDA drug labels for DMD medications*)
*   **Patient Resources:** Numerous patient advocacy organizations, such as the Parent Project Muscular Dystrophy (PPMD) and the Muscular Dystrophy Association (MDA), provide valuable resources and support for patients and families affected by DMD. (*Source: Parent Project Muscular Dystrophy (PPMD)*)
*   **Research:** Ongoing research focuses on developing new therapies, including gene therapy and personalized medicine approaches, to address the underlying genetic defect in DMD. (*Source: ClinicalTrials.gov: Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy*)
