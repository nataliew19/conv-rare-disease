Q: Provide an informative entry about Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy.
A: Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) is a rare genetic neuromuscular disorder that primarily affects males [1][2]. It is characterized by progressive muscle degeneration and weakness due to mutations in the dystrophin gene [2][3]. This gene is responsible for producing dystrophin, a protein essential for maintaining the integrity of muscle cells [1][2]. DMD affects approximately 1 in 3,500 to 5,000 male births worldwide [1][2].

**Symptoms and Progression:** DMD symptom onset typically occurs in early childhood, between the ages of 2 and 3 [2]. In the early stages, DMD affects the shoulder and upper arm muscles, as well as the muscles of the hips and thighs, leading to difficulties in rising from the floor, climbing stairs, maintaining balance, and raising the arms [2]. As the disease progresses, muscle weakness and atrophy spread to affect the trunk and forearms and gradually progress to involve additional muscles of the body [1]. Affected individuals may require a wheelchair by their teenage years [1]. Serious life-threatening complications, such as cardiomyopathy and respiratory difficulties, may develop [1].

**Diagnosis:** DMD is suspected when muscle weakness and high creatine kinase levels are detected [3]. Genetic testing can confirm the diagnosis and identify the disease-causing mutation in the dystrophin gene [2].

**Treatment and Management:** While there is no known cure for DMD [1][4], treatment aims to manage symptoms and improve quality of life [4]. Consensus statements recommend early commencement of steroid treatment [3]. Other treatments include respiratory management, heart failure treatment, nutritional management, and rehabilitation [3].

**Life Expectancy:** Life expectancy for people with DMD has increased significantly in recent years [5]. The median life expectancy for individuals born after 1990 is approximately 28.1 years [5]. Advances in treatment and supportive care have helped many live longer than once expected [4].

**Current Research:** Current research is focused on developing therapies to target the pathology of DMD or improve muscle growth and regeneration [3]. These therapies include cell-based therapy, gene therapy, antisense oligonucleotide therapy, myostatin inhibition, and stop codon read-through therapy [3]. A new single-dose intravenous gene therapy, delandistrogene moxeparvovec, creates “micro-dystrophin” and targets the skeletal and heart muscles [6].
